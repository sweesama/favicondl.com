Squarespace Favicon Not Showing on Google? The Fix

You uploaded your brand icon weeks ago, but your Squarespace favicon is not showing on Google search results. You check the browser tab—it looks perfect. You check Google—and there is a blank space where your brand should be. This discrepancy is a known friction point for Squarespace users.

The delay usually comes down to how Google crawls and caches icons versus how Squarespace serves them. Squarespace relies on a dynamic CDN for static assets, which creates a mismatch with Google's indexing expectations.

The Quick Fix: Force a Google Recrawl

If your favicon is visible in the browser tab but missing from Google, the fastest fix is to force Google to recrawl your homepage. Google does not update favicons in real-time; it pulls them during routine indexing passes.

Use Google Search Console to request indexing for your root domain. Enter your homepage URL into the URL Inspection tool. If the page is already indexed, click the 'Request Indexing' button. This action pushes Googlebot to re-evaluate your page structure and linked assets.

Before you do this, verify that Squarespace is actually injecting the favicon link tag into your live HTML. Right-click your published site, select 'View Page Source', and search for favicon. You should see a tag pointing to a static1.squarespace.com URL.

Why Google Ignores Squarespace Favicons

If you forced a recrawl and the icon still is not showing, you need to look at how Google validates favicons. Google has documented requirements for favicons in search results. Squarespace's default implementation sometimes conflicts with these rules.

Here are the most common root causes for this specific platform:

  • CDN URL instability: Squarespace generates dynamic URLs for uploaded assets. If you re-upload or change your favicon in the site settings, the underlying CDN URL changes completely. Google caches the old URL, hits a 404, and drops your icon.
  • Missing root-level favicon.ico: Googlebot historically checks the site root for a favicon.ico file. Squarespace does not place your uploaded icon at the root domain. It serves it from a subdomain CDN. If Googlebot checks the root first and fails, it may delay indexing the CDN link.
  • Crawl budget delays: If your Squarespace site is new or has low traffic, Googlebot might not revisit frequently enough to catch your recently uploaded icon.
  • Invalid file dimensions: Google requires a square favicon. If you uploaded a rectangular logo in Squarespace, the platform crops it. If the resulting square is smaller than 48x48 pixels, Google will reject it for search results.

Verifying Your Squarespace Favicon Setup

You need to ensure your uploaded file meets Google's criteria before requesting another crawl. Google requires a square image of at least 8x8 pixels, but recommends 48x48 pixels or larger. For more detail on these requirements, read our guide on favicon not showing in Google search.

Check your uploaded file in Squarespace by going to Design > Browser Icon. If you uploaded a transparent PNG, verify that it does not blend into the white background of Google's search results page. A dark or vibrant background works best for visibility.

Fixing the CDN Caching Trap

The biggest hurdle with Squarespace is the dynamic CDN URL. When you update your favicon in the Squarespace dashboard, the platform assigns a new static URL with a unique identifier. The old URL immediately stops resolving.

If Google previously cached your old favicon URL, it will attempt to fetch that URL on its next crawl. When it hits a 404 error, Google drops your favicon from search results entirely. It will not automatically search your HTML for the new link tag unless it recrawls the page.

To fix this, you must ensure Google knows your HTML structure has changed. After updating your browser icon in Squarespace:

  1. Wait 24 hours for the CDN to propagate the new file globally.
  2. Inspect your page source to confirm the new static1.squarespace.com URL is present.
  3. Submit the root domain for indexing in Google Search Console.
  4. Wait 3 to 7 days for Googlebot to process the new HTML and cache the new CDN URL.

Preventing Future Favicon Drops

Once your icon appears in Google search, you want to keep it there. The most common reason for a favicon to disappear from Google after a successful setup is a theme change or image swap in Squarespace.

Every time you change your browser icon in Squarespace, you break the existing CDN URL. Google eventually catches up, but there is a gap where your brand looks unprofessional in search results. Avoid changing your favicon unless absolutely necessary.

If you must update your icon, keep the dimensions identical to the previous file. A sudden change from a square PNG to a rectangular one can cause Squarespace to serve a broken link temporarily, which Googlebot might cache.

When to Wait vs. When to Act

Google's indexing pipeline is slow. If you just published your Squarespace site yesterday, the favicon will not appear in Google immediately. It takes days for the initial crawl and additional time for Google to process the image and display it in SERPs.

If it has been less than a week since your site went live, wait. Check the URL Inspection tool in Search Console to confirm the page is indexed. If the page is indexed but the favicon is still missing after two weeks, run through the CDN verification steps above.

If the URL Inspection tool shows your homepage is not indexed at all, your favicon will not show up. You have a larger indexing problem to solve first. Squarespace sites sometimes block Googlebot by default if you have 'Disable search engine indexing' enabled in your settings. Check Settings > SEO and ensure the 'Hide from search engines' toggle is off.

Fixing a missing favicon on Google requires patience. Squarespace's CDN architecture means you cannot force a permanent root-level favicon.ico file, so you are dependent on Googlebot reading your HTML link tags correctly. Verify your source code, request a recrawl, and give the search engine time to process the new asset.
