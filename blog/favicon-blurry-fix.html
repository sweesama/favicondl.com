Why Is My Favicon Blurry? 7 Causes and Quick Fixes

You've uploaded what looks like a sharp 1024×1024 PNG, but the favicon is still blurry in the browser tab. We've all seen this. The culprit is almost always a mismatch between the file you uploaded and the file the browser picks to render — not a flaw in your source artwork.

The 2-Minute Quick Fix

Most blurry favicons resolve with one workflow. Run this before chasing deeper causes.

  1. Open Mzu favicondl and upload your master image (an SVG or at least a 512×512 PNG).
  2. Generate the full package: a 16×16, 32×32, and 48×48 multi-size ICO, a 180×180 Apple Touch Icon, and 192×192 and 512×512 PNGs for your web manifest.
  3. Replace every favicon file in your site root and /icons directory. Don't leave old files lying around.
  4. Hard reload your browser (Cmd-Shift-R on macOS, Ctrl-Shift-R on Windows).
  5. Verify with DevTools → Network → filter favicon and confirm the new file is actually served.

Add this minimal stack to your HTML head:

<link rel='icon' href='/favicon.ico' sizes='16x16 32x32 48x48'>
<link rel='icon' type='image/svg+xml' href='/favicon.svg'>
<link rel='icon' type='image/png' sizes='32x32' href='/favicon-32.png'>

If the tab still looks fuzzy after this, the cause is one of the seven below.

Why Your Favicon Is Blurry: 7 Root Causes

1. Your browser is upscaling a 16×16 file on a retina screen

The most common scenario: you ship a favicon.ico containing only a 16×16 image. On a 1× display it looks fine. On a retina (2× or 3×) screen, the OS picks that 16×16 file and scales it up with bilinear filtering. That's where the mush comes from. Embed 16, 32, and 48 into a single multi-size ICO and the OS will pick the sharpest match — see the multi-size ICO guide.

2. You shipped a single huge PNG

A 512×512 PNG is great for the home-screen manifest, but it's the wrong tool for the tab. If you only declare that PNG in your <link rel="icon">, the browser downsamples it to 16 or 32 logical pixels. Bilinear downsampling of photographic content always looks soft. You need a dedicated 32×32 (or larger, pixel-aligned) PNG to anchor the tab render.

3. Your SVG is missing a viewBox

An SVG without a viewBox attribute gets rasterized at whatever size the browser chooses — usually 16×16 — then upscaled to fill the tab slot. Crisp Bézier paths turn into muddy outlines. Always declare viewBox="0 0 32 32" (or similar) and design within that grid. The SVG favicon guide walks through the full pattern.

4. You embedded raster art inside an SVG

Designers paste a JPEG or PNG into Illustrator and save as SVG. Browsers render this happily, but at 16×16 the raster gets sampled, not vector-scaled. The scalable promise of SVG is gone. Convert raster fills to actual vector paths, or skip the SVG entirely and ship a multi-size ICO instead.

5. Your ICO contains only one resolution

ICO is a container format, not a single image. If you renamed a 16×16 PNG to .ico, that's exactly what you have — a 16×16-only file. Operating systems and browsers pick the largest embedded size that fits the slot. With only 16×16 available, every higher-DPI render is a soft upscale.

6. Your logo has too much detail for 16×16

Thin strokes, five-pointed stars, gradients, fine serifs — all of these become invisible or muddy at tab size. The icon isn't blurry in a technical sense; it just carries more information than the pixel grid can express. Simplify to a single bold mark with thick strokes and high contrast.

7. Your browser is showing a cached old version

Chrome's favicon cache is famously sticky. Even after you upload a new file, Chrome may keep serving the old one across tab reloads, especially on macOS. Safari caches even harder. The cure is a hard reload plus, in stubborn cases, manually clearing the favicon cache.

How to Prevent a Blurry Favicon

  • Always ship a true multi-size ICO with 16, 32, and 48 embedded.
  • Provide an SVG with a proper viewBox for modern browsers.
  • Provide a 180×180 PNG for Apple Touch.
  • Provide 192×192 and 512×512 PNGs in your web manifest.
  • Design on a 32×32 grid first, then scale up to larger sizes.
  • Test on actual retina hardware, not just your designer's monitor.
  • Bust the cache with a query string on deploys: /favicon.ico?v=2.
