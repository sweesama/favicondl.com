Google Favicon 48×48: Requirement or Recommendation?

The phrase “Google favicon requirements 48x48” is widely searched, but it often gets repeated incorrectly. Google's current rule is not “upload exactly a 48×48 PNG.”

According to the official Google Search documentation, a favicon must be square and at least 8×8 pixels. Google recommends an image larger than 48×48 pixels so it can look good across surfaces. That is a recommendation, not an exact-size requirement.

What Google actually requires

  • The image must have a 1:1 aspect ratio.
  • It must be at least 8×8 pixels; larger than 48×48 is recommended.
  • Google supports valid favicon formats.
  • Googlebot must be able to crawl the home page and Googlebot-Image must be able to crawl the icon.
  • The favicon URL should remain stable.
  • Google supports one favicon per site, where a site is defined by hostname.

Google may still choose not to show the favicon. Compliance creates eligibility; it does not create a display guarantee.

What Google does not require

The same documentation does not specify a 3KB maximum, a mandatory 48×48 PNG, a requirement to include 48×48 in a web manifest, or a published manifest-first discovery hierarchy. Those claims should not be presented as Google rules.

A valid 64×64, 96×96, or scalable icon can satisfy the “larger than 48×48” recommendation. Choose a size and format your site can serve reliably, then test the artwork at the small display size where people will see it.

A practical setup

<link rel='icon' href='/favicon.svg' type='image/svg+xml' sizes='any'>
<link rel='icon' href='/favicon.ico' sizes='any'>

This is an implementation example, not a special Google ranking recipe. The important parts are a valid declaration on the home page, crawlable files, stable URLs, and recognizable artwork.

Design for recognition, not an imaginary grid

Small text and thin details are difficult to recognize in any search interface. Simplifying the mark and checking contrast is sensible design advice, but it should not be described as proof of higher click-through rate.

After deployment, use the verified Google favicon checklist to inspect the live file and request recrawling. Google says processing can take several days to several weeks.
